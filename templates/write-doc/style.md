# Writing style

These rules apply to papers, documents, READMEs and other written work. Chat replies are outside this file.

## Banned patterns (papers and documents)

- Never use em dashes. Use a period, a comma, a colon or parentheses.
- Never use contractions. Write "do not", "it is", "cannot", "will not". This includes every form, including "let us" in place of the short form.
- Never use litotes. Do not say "not bad" or "not unlike". Say the direct thing: "good" or "similar".
- Never use irony or sarcasm. Say what you mean in plain words.

## Banned AI-signal words and patterns (papers and documents)

Readers and detectors now treat these words as marks of machine writing. Do not use them. Choose a plain word, or state the fact directly.

- **Verbs:** delve, underscore, showcase, highlight (as filler), emphasize, elucidate, encompass, facilitate, foster, enhance, bolster, garner, leverage, harness, navigate (abstract use), streamline, unveil, illuminate, orchestrate, pinpoint, resonate, align with, boast, exemplify.
- **Adjectives:** crucial, pivotal, vital, key (as empty emphasis), intricate, meticulous, multifaceted, comprehensive, robust, seamless, transformative, groundbreaking, innovative, invaluable, unparalleled, noteworthy, remarkable, vibrant, rich, profound, enduring, nuanced, holistic, cutting-edge.
- **Nouns:** tapestry, landscape (abstract), realm, testament, interplay, intricacies, nuances, insights, avenue, beacon, cornerstone, paradigm, synergy, journey (abstract), game-changer.
- **Filler and transitions:** additionally, moreover, furthermore, in conclusion, in summary, overall, it is important to note, it is worth noting, in today's world, in the ever-evolving, in the realm of, at the end of the day.
- **Promotional phrases:** nestled, in the heart of, stands as, serves as a testament, plays a pivotal role, sets the stage, turning point, indelible mark, commitment to, diverse array, rich tapestry.
- **Structures:** "not just X, but Y" and "not only X but also Y" (state Y directly). Forced groups of three. A closing paragraph that restates the text. A "challenges and future outlook" ending. Opening with a broad claim about the modern era.

If a plain synonym is just as inflated, rewrite the sentence so the fact carries the weight. Say what a thing does, how many, when and why. Use "is" and "has" instead of "serves as" and "boasts". Some of these words are fine in a direct quote or a technical term that has no substitute. Name the exception when it applies.

## Structural and tonal AI tells (papers, documents, READMEs)

Avoid every item below in written work. The check script cannot check most of them, so check your own draft against this list before you finish.

**Formatting**
- Use sentence case in headings, unless the project style says otherwise.
- Use bold rarely. Do not bold a lead-in phrase on every bullet or paragraph.
- Do not use emojis.
- Do not put horizontal rules between sections.
- Do not skip heading levels.
- Do not turn every topic into a bullet list. Write paragraphs when the ideas connect.
- Do not default to exactly three or five items in a list. Use the number the content has.
- Leave no markdown leftovers, such as stray asterisks, unclosed brackets or citation stubs like "[cite: 1]".
- Use straight quotation marks in plain text and markdown files.

**Sentence and paragraph structure**
- Do not use "not just X, but Y", "not only X but also Y" or "X rather than Y" as a device. State the point directly.
- Do not group ideas in threes by reflex.
- Do not end sentences with a participle phrase that adds fake analysis, such as "highlighting the importance of", "reflecting the need for" or "contributing to the growth of".
- Do not use false ranges such as "from X to Y" when X and Y are not ends of one scale.
- Do not vary a name for style. Use the same word for the same thing.
- Do not end with an outline-style conclusion: "Despite these challenges", "Future outlook", "Challenges and legacy".
- Do not end with a paragraph that restates the text. End when the content ends.
- Use "is", "has" and "does" in place of "serves as", "stands as", "boasts" and "features".
- Use direct connections in place of "in connection with" and "associated with".

**Tone**
- Do not use promotional language for a neutral subject.
- Do not claim significance without evidence. Say what happened, how much and to whom.
- Do not use vague attribution such as "observers say", "experts agree" or "industry reports". Name the source or drop the claim.
- Do not editorialize with "it is important to note".
- Do not flatter or agree by reflex: no "great question".

**Chatbot leftovers**
- Do not write "I hope this helps", "let me know if you need more" or any offer of further help inside a document.
- Do not add knowledge-cutoff disclaimers or "as an AI" statements.
- Do not leave placeholder text such as "this section would describe".

Sources for this list: the Wikipedia guide "Signs of AI writing" and the excess-vocabulary study by Kobak and colleagues on 15 million PubMed abstracts.

## Register for papers and documents

Write with flow. Sentence limits for chat replies do not apply here.
- Keep a matter-of-fact voice. State what is true, what happened and what to do. Skip filler, praise and hedging.
- Make the writing interesting and continuous. Link sentences so each one leads to the next. Use a concrete detail, a cause or a consequence to hold the reader's attention.
- Vary sentence length. Mix short and longer sentences, and use transitions such as "so", "because", "however" and "as a result" where the ideas connect.
- Prefer connected paragraphs over lists when the ideas relate. Use a list only for steps or parallel items.

If a tool or template forces one of the banned patterns, write the text without it and say so.
